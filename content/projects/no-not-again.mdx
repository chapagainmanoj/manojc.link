No Not Again is a lightweight Chrome and Edge extension with two jobs: it stops you from opening
distracting sites with a note you wrote to yourself, and it keeps notes for any website you visit.
Everything stays in your browser.

[Install from the Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/no-not-again/elipenocngmniimdbfnhignnbojkdmpe)

## Block distractions with your own words

Pick a site you keep drifting to. Next time you open it, No Not Again pauses on a calm page that
shows your note, a motivational quote, or both. Go back, or continue for an hour, six hours, a day
or a week. Blocking resumes on its own when the time is up.

## Keep notes for any website

Open the side panel from the toolbar, press <kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>N</kbd>, or
right-click a page and choose **Add note**. Notes belong to the site, so they show up on every page
of it, and the toolbar icon shows how many there are. Search across all your notes, and export them
to a file for backup.

## Privacy

No Not Again does not collect, transmit, or store any personal, browsing, or behavioral data on any
server operated by the developer. It has no analytics and makes no network requests of its own.

- **Blocked sites and quotes** (the site name, your note, the display setting, and your own quotes)
  are saved with Chrome's sync storage. If Chrome Sync is turned on, Chrome syncs them to your Google
  account so they're available on your other devices. Google handles this under its own privacy
  policy, and the developer never has access to them.
- **Site notes** are stored only on your device, together with the name of the site each note
  belongs to (for example `github.com`). They are never synced or sent anywhere. Export files are
  created only when you ask for one and are saved wherever you choose.
- **Temporary bypass settings** are stored locally on your device and are never synced.
- The extension reads the address of the tab you're visiting only to check whether you've blocked
  that site or saved notes for it, and to show the note count. Page addresses are never stored or
  shared.
